# Sources

- "You can't tickle yourself" / brain predicts own touch, cancels response, someone else's touch not predicted, delay makes it tickly:
  https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/why-cant-a-person-tickle/ (Sarah-Jayne Blakemore, UCL)
  - "the cerebellum can predict sensations when your own movement causes them but not when someone else does"
  - "this prediction is used to cancel the response of other brain areas to the tickle"
  - "Further studies using robots showed that the presence of a small delay between your own movement and the resulting tickle can make the sensation feel tickly" (also: "the longer the delay, the more tickly it feels")
- fMRI study comparing self-tickling with being tickled by another person (Blakemore, Wolpert, Frith):
  https://staff.washington.edu/chudler/funny.html
  - "They used fMRI to peer into the brains of subjects while the people were tickling themselves"

Note: last line of the script ("only mutes touches it saw coming") is a plain summary of the prediction-cancel claim above. "Spoils the surprise" is framing, not a separate claim.
