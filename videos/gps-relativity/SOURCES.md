# Sources

Opened: https://www.astronomy.ohio-state.edu/pogge.1/Ast162/Unit5/gps.html (Ohio State Astronomy, Ast162 course notes)

- Drift ~10 km/day: "errors in global positions would continue to accumulate at a rate of about 10 kilometers each day"
- Orbit ~20,000 km: "Each satellite in the GPS constellation orbits at an altitude of about 20,000 km from the ground"
- Satellite clocks run fast, 38 µs/day net: "the clocks on-board each satellite should tick faster than identical clocks on the ground by about 38 microseconds per day"
- Weaker gravity makes them run fast (+45 µs; speed costs -7 µs): "the clocks in each GPS satellite should get ahead of ground-based clocks by 45 microseconds per day"

Not verified (cut from script): the pre-launch frequency offset fix; "hundreds of km in a week".
Radio signals travel at light speed: general physics, GPS ranging by signal timing; not separately sourced here.
