# Sources

- Ten days removed; Oct 4 -> Oct 15, 1582: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorian_calendar — "corrected by a deletion of 10 days"; the reform moved the calendar from Thursday 4 October to Friday 15 October.
- Julian year too long: same page — the Julian 365.25-day year was "an overestimate of a little under one day per century", so the calendar drifted against the seasons.
- Leap rule: same page — "every year divisible by four is a leap year, except for years that are divisible by 100 but not also divisible by 400."
- Average year 365.2425 vs solar ~365.2422: same page — average year is "365+97/400, or 365.2425, days long", closely matching the solar year of about 365.2422 days.
- Why leap years exist: https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/leap-year/en/ — "Leap years are important so that our calendar year matches the solar year."
- 1900 / 2000: derived from the leap rule above (1900 divisible by 100, not 400; 2000 divisible by 400).
