# Sources (sun-vanishes)

Quotes below were seen in pages fetched this session. "Outline-only" means the claim comes from outline.json and my fetch did not find the sentence; re-check before publishing.

- Sun ~150 million km / 93 million miles; more than 330,000 Earths in mass; 99.8% of solar system mass. https://science.nasa.gov/sun/facts/ — "It's about 93 million miles (150 million kilometers) from Earth"; "it would take more than 330,000 Earths to match the mass of the Sun"; "our Sun, which accounts for 99.8% of our solar system's mass."
- Light delay ~8.3 min for 1 AU. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_of_gravity — table row "from Sun to Earth (1 AU) 8.3 min".
- Gravity and light changes arrive after ~8 minutes (question and thesis). https://www.astronomy.com/science/if-the-sun-suddenly-disappeared-it-would-take-about-eight-minutes-for-earth-to-become-dark-due-to-the-speed-of-light-how-long-would-it-take-to-feel-the-absence-of-the-suns-gravity/ — "...in the same amount of time it takes to see the lack of sunlight — about 8 minutes."
- Gravity travels at light speed; thought experiment. https://wtamu.edu/~cbaird/sq/2013/04/30/what-is-the-speed-of-gravity/ — "Gravity travels at the speed of light in vacuum."; "Both of these changes would propagate out to the earth at the speed of light in vacuum."
- General relativity / GW170817 confirms speed equals c. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_of_gravity — "...confirmed by observation of the GW170817 neutron star merger, is equal to the speed of light (c)."
- Speed-difference bound and 14 orders of magnitude. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GW170817 — "...is constrained to be between −3×10^−15 and +7×10^−16 times the speed of light, which improves on the previous estimate by about 14 orders of magnitude."
- GW170817 timing, ~130 million years ago, detected by LIGO and Virgo. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GW170817 — infobox: "130 million years ago (detected 17 August 2017)", "Instrument LIGO, Virgo". The 1.7 second gamma-ray delay is outline-only (not found in my fetch).
- Earth orbital speed 29.78 km/s; average temperature 288 K (15 C). https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/earthfact.html — "Mean orbital velocity (km/s) 29.78"; "Average temperature: 288 K (15 C)".
- Straight-line departure at ~30 km/s (67,000 mph). https://www.sciencefocus.com/space/what-would-happen-to-earths-orbit-if-the-sun-vanished/ — "...it would head off towards the stars at about 30km/s (67,000mph)."
- Moon goes dark; humans freeze within weeks. Outline-only (sciencefocus page, sentence not found in my fetch).
- Caveat that some observations are argued to be in tension with equal speeds. Outline-only (Speed_of_gravity page).
