# Sources
Opened: Lafer-Sousa, Hermann & Conway, "Striking individual differences in color perception uncovered by 'the dress' photograph", Current Biology 2015 (PDF text extracted and read): https://www1.wellesley.edu/sites/default/files/assets/departments/neuroscience/images/lafersousahermannconway_thedress_2015_withsupp-1.pdf
- Survey of 1,401 people: "We confirmed this in a survey of 1,401 subjects (313 naïve; 53 tested in laboratory; 28/53 re-tested)."
- 57% / 30%: "Overall, 57% of subjects described the dress as blue/black (B/K); 30% as white/gold (W/G); 11% as blue/brown (B/B)"
- Brain guesses the light: "A color percept is the visual system's best guess given available sense data and an internal model of the world."
- Cool light, white/gold: "Some people may expect a cool illuminant, discount short wavelengths, and perceive white/gold"
- Warm light, blue/black: "others may favor a warm illuminant, discount longer wavelengths, and see blue/black."
- Phrased as "scientists think" because the paper says "We hypothesize"; a 2015 Gegenfurtner et al. commentary (found via search, not opened) offers a related but different account.
